Mushrooms have long played a significant role in nutrition, economics, and global food security. However, contamination by molds such as Trichoderma, Aspergillus, and Rhizopus remains a persistent challenge, particularly during the incubation period of fruiting bags. Consequently, traditional manual inspection methods are often subjective and insufficient for early contamination detection, resulting in substantial yield losses. To address this problem, the study aimed to develop an image-based contamination classification framework capable of accurately identifying healthy and contaminated mushroom fruiting bags. Accordingly, a dataset comprising healthy, green mold, black mold fruiting bags was collected from mushroom farms in Laguna. Images were captured from multiple angles using an iPhone 15 and subsequently validated by experienced mushroom growers and agricultural experts to ensure accurate class representation. Subsequently, the dataset underwent preprocessing procedures, including data cleaning, resizing to 224 × 224 pixels, augmentation, and annotation. The classification model integrated deep features extracted from a modified ResNet-18 architecture enhanced with a Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) and handcrafted texture features obtained from the Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM). These features were concatenated and input into an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) classifier, which was trained over 100 epochs. Evaluation results indicated that the model achieved an overall accuracy of 86.9% in distinguishing between healthy and contaminated fruiting bags, while generating expert validated recommendations for each contamination type.
